## overview

halftone is a small command-line tool that converts images into ascii art. it
started as a weekend experiment in floyd-steinberg dithering and slowly turned
into something i actually use — a way to make screenshots feel less precious
when they end up in a readme.

## how it works

the renderer walks the source image in tiles, samples luminance per tile, and
maps the result to a curated glyph ramp. the ramp matters more than the
algorithm — wider ramps look noisy at small sizes, so the default is just
twelve characters, hand-picked.

## design notes

- typography first. glyph choice and line-height matter more than resolution.
- no color. greyscale forces honest contrast decisions.
- single binary. drop it anywhere, run it on anything.

## what i learned

writing a small tool teaches you where the real complexity lives. dithering
was easy. font metrics, terminal color spaces, and pipe buffering were not.
the lesson, repeated: the interesting problems are at the seams.
